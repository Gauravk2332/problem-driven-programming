Chapter 02 / 07

Understand the greedy rule

Sort by descending profit. Place each job in the latest free slot at or before its deadline. Latest placement preserves earlier slots for tighter jobs. If no slot exists, reject. Slots number 1..min(n,max deadline); larger deadlines can be capped because at most n jobs fit.

Total 142. Greedy correctness: feasible job sets satisfy the exchange property. A set is schedulable exactly when the number of its jobs with deadline ≤ t is at most t for every t (sort selected jobs by deadline). For feasible sets X,Y with |X|<|Y|, take the largest saturated prefix deadline t of X (or 0 if none). Y has at most t jobs due by t while X has t; since Y is larger, some job of Y outside X has deadline >t and can be added without violating any prefix constraint. Thus these sets form a matroid; descending-profit feasible insertion is optimal by exchange. Latest-slot insertion is an exact feasibility test: every job occupying a slot chose the latest free slot, so if all slots ≤d are full, repeatedly extend the full prefix to the maximum deadlines of its occupants until stable; adding this job violates that saturated prefix. No unit-time assumption, no such guarantee in general.

Implement and analyse

def sequence(jobs):
    d=min(len(jobs),max((x[0] for x in jobs),default=0))
    slots=[None]*d;profit=0
    for deadline,p in sorted(jobs,key=lambda x:x[1],reverse=True):
        for t in range(min(d,deadline)-1,-1,-1):
            if slots[t] is None:
                slots[t]=p;profit+=p;break
    return profit
print(sequence([(2,100),(1,19),(2,27),(1,25),(3,15)]))

O(n log n+nD) time and O(D) slots, D=min(n,max deadline), plus sorting storage. Worst O(n²) scan. A disjoint-set predecessor structure can accelerate finding free slots. Zero-profit jobs are optional; skip negative profits if allowed.
