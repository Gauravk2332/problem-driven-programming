Kruskal

sort edges by weight
for (u, v, w): if find(u) ≠ find(v):
  accept; union(u, v)
stop at V − 1 edges

O(E log E); DSU ≈ O(1) per op.

Example (cost 39)

AD5 ✓ CE5 ✓ DF6 ✓ AB7 ✓ BE7 ✓ BC8 ✗ EF8 ✗ BD9 ✗ EG9 ✓ ⇒ 6 edges, 39.

Cut property

Lightest edge e across cut (S, V−S) is in some MST: T + e has a cycle with another crossing f, w(f) ≥ w(e) ⇒ T − f + e is an MST.

Two-mark self-test

  1. Define a minimum spanning tree.
    A spanning tree (connects all V vertices with V − 1 edges, no cycles) of minimum total edge weight in a connected weighted undirected graph.
  2. State the cut property.
    For any cut (S, V − S), a minimum-weight edge crossing it belongs to some MST.
  3. Name four techniques for proving greedy algorithms correct.
    Greedy stays ahead; exchange argument; lower bound (structural bound); cut property.
  4. What does DSU provide in Kruskal's algorithm?
    Fast find (same component?) and union (merge components), almost O(1) amortised each with union by size and path compression.
  5. Give the time complexity of Kruskal's algorithm.
    O(E log E) for sorting, plus near-linear DSU work: O(E log E) overall.
