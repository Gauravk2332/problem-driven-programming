Problem Driven Programming · R1UC544B
Lecture 30

Idea

Store only the past information that changes future choices. Impossible states start at −∞, never 0. Update every state from the previous pair (simultaneous assignment).

Non-adjacent selection

skip'=max(skip,take)
take'=skip+v
start (0,−∞); answer max(skip,take)

[2,7,9,3,1] → 12 (2+9+1). [4,5,4] → 8: greedy 5 fails. Circular row: max of [0..n−2] and [1..n−1].

Stock, unlimited trades

cash'=max(cash,hold+p)
hold'=max(hold,cash−p)
start (0,−∞); answer cash

[7,1,5,3,6,4] → 7: buy 1 sell 5, buy 3 sell 6. hold is net profit while owning a share, not market value.

Changed rules

fee: cash'=max(cash,hold+p−f)
cooldown: rest'=max(rest,sold), sold'=hold+p, hold'=max(hold,rest−p)

Cooldown start (0,−∞,−∞); answer max(rest,sold); [1,2,3,0,2] → 3. Limited trades: add a counter.

Complexity · full marks

Θ(n) time, Θ(1) extra for the value; store decisions to recover the chosen houses or trades.

  • In-place updates can allow an illegal second action.
  • Draw the transition graph first.

Full marks: states, transitions, base, answer, trace.

Two-mark self-test

  1. Why use −∞ in initial take/hold?
    No valid history reaches that state before any action; it must never win a maximum.
  2. What is the correct terminal state for trading?
    Cash, since no share may remain unsold.
