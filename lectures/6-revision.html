Definitions

Prime: n > 1 with divisors only 1, n. 1 is not prime; 2 is the only even prime. FTA: unique factorisation, 360 = 2³·3²·5.

Trial division

for d = 2 while d ≤ n/d: if n mod d = 0 → composite

Composite ⇒ factor ≤ √n. Θ(√n) time. 97: d = 2…9, prime.

Sieve of Eratosthenes

for p = 2 while p² ≤ N:
  if p unmarked: mark p², p²+p, … ≤ N

Start at p² (smaller multiples already marked); stop at √N. Θ(N log log N) time, Θ(N) space.

SPF factorisation

spf[n] = smallest prime factor. 360 → 180 → 90 → 45 → 15 → 5 → 1. O(log n) per number.

Common mistakes · full marks

  • 1 is not prime.
  • d·d ≤ n can overflow; use d ≤ n/d.
  • Sieve memory Θ(N): segment for huge N.

Full marks: algorithm, both justifications, trace, complexity.

Two-mark self-test

  1. Define a prime number.
    An integer greater than 1 whose only positive divisors are 1 and itself.
  2. Why is trial division up to √n sufficient?
    If n = ab with 1 < a ≤ b, then a ≤ √n; otherwise ab > n.
  3. State the time and space complexity of the sieve.
    Time Θ(N log log N); space Θ(N).
  4. Why does the sieve start crossing from p²?
    Multiples kp with k < p have a smaller prime factor and are already crossed.
  5. State the fundamental theorem of arithmetic.
    Every integer > 1 is a product of primes, uniquely up to order.
